# Voice and tone

One voice across the marketing pages, the portal and the extension: **clear,
professional, specific, confident, goal-oriented.**

The reader is not "organising a job search". They are going after a particular
job and want it. Every line should sound like it was written by someone who
takes that goal seriously.

## The five rules

**1. Clear.** One idea per sentence, in the order the reader needs it. No
metaphors that need decoding, no jokes that cost a re-read.

**2. Professional.** Sentence case. No exclamation marks, no "Oops", no
cheerleading. Contractions are welcome ("what's", "you're") — stiffness is not
professionalism.

**3. Specific.** Name the thing. "LinkedIn, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday",
"row-level security on every table", "under a minute" — not "many sites",
"secure", "fast".

**4. Confident.** State what the product does. Never hedge ("might help you",
"we hope"), never apologise for a feature, and never argue against a claim in
the copy beside it.

**5. Goal-oriented.** Frame everything as movement towards the offer, not as
relief from a chore. Say what the reader gets, not what they avoid.

| Instead of                                      | Write                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Built for people running a real job search      | Built for people going after the job they want          |
| Nothing slips between applying and hearing back | Stay in front of every employer, from applying to offer |
| Follow-ups that never slip                      | Today's follow-ups, first in line                       |
| Know whether the search is actually working     | See exactly what's moving you closer to an offer        |
| Clip a posting before it disappears             | Clip a posting the moment you find it                   |
| Stop rebuilding the spreadsheet                 | Every offer starts with one tracked application         |
| No stale tabs, no guessing                      | One board, always current                               |

The pattern in that table: the left column is defined by what goes wrong, the
right column by what the reader is trying to achieve. Negation ("nothing", "no",
"never", "stop", "before it's gone") is the tell. When a line needs it twice,
the sentence is arguing with a fear rather than making a promise — rewrite it
from the goal.

## Applying it

- **Headings** make a claim. **Ledes** say concretely how it is met.
- **Empty states** point at the next action, in the reader's terms: "Your
  numbers appear here as soon as you log the first role you're going after."
- **Toasts** report the fact and stop: "Signed in.", "Task added", "Profile
  saved". No exclamation marks.
- **Errors** say what happened and what to do — never blame the reader.
- **Meta descriptions** carry the same positioning as the page they describe.
  The site-wide line lives in `src/routes/__root.tsx`; keep per-page overrides
  consistent with it.
- **Legal and disclaimer copy** (the logo strip's attribution line) stays plain
  and unedited by tone work. Accuracy outranks voice there.
